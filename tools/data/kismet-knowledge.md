# Kismet — facts the copywriter may use
Source: kismetpets.com (Ingredients page, Our Story, "What's in the Bowl", "We Clinically Tested Kismet", comparison guide, switching guide). Use ONLY these facts for product, health and brand claims. Never invent numbers, studies, awards, prices, ingredients or health claims. If the brief needs a fact that is not here, write around it.

## The food
- Developed with board-certified veterinary nutritionists and expert formulators. Complete and balanced for all life stages (puppy, adult, senior), per AAFCO guidelines.
- Real animal protein is the #1 ingredient (chicken or salmon). No animal by-product meals.
- No corn, no wheat, no fillers, no artificial flavors, colors or preservatives.
- Antioxidant-rich superfoods, at meaningful inclusion ("the fruits and vegetables aren't just pixie dust — everything in the formula has a job").
- Pre- and probiotics in both dry recipes, added after cooking, for digestive health; supporting the gut shows up in skin, coat and mobility.
- Freeze-dried nuggets ("Nugs") mixed in: real meat pieces for extra protein and flavor. The dry food is complete and balanced even without them.
- Recipes: Chicken & Barley; Salmon & Brown Rice. Dry, shelf-stable: no fridge, no freezer, no prep.
- Why dry instead of raw/fresh: the safest, most proven process; raw has microbial-control issues, fresh has more recall risk and needs refrigeration, and neither is nutritionally better — at a much higher price.
- How it is made: dry ingredients blended (brown rice, sweet potato, dried carrots), fresh meat or fish added to form a dough, cooked (target 200°F), cut into pieces, dried from ~22% to ~10% moisture, then coated with fat, natural flavors and the pre/probiotics.

## Ingredients and what each one does
- Real Chicken: good source of energy; builds muscle mass; loaded with amino acids.
- Real Salmon: highly digestible protein; rich in omega-3s and B vitamins; helps maintain muscle mass; supports a healthy, shiny coat.
- Barley: supports healthy digestion; promotes heart health.
- Brown Rice: source of fiber and B vitamins; supports healthy digestion and sustained energy.
- Beet Pulp: high-quality source of fiber; maintains intestinal health; enhances ingredient absorption.
- Sweet Potato: highly digestible, tasty source of fiber; essential vitamins and antioxidants.
- Carrots: rich in beta-carotene and vitamin A; helps maintain a healthy immune system; supports eyes, skin and coat.
- Kale: rich in calcium, magnesium, potassium and iron; helps maintain bone and heart health.
- Apples: source of vitamin C, fiber and antioxidants; helps maintain overall health.
- Cranberries: antioxidant rich; essential vitamins and minerals; helps maintain a healthy immune system.
- Pre & Probiotics: promote a healthy gut biome; support digestion and overall vitality.
- Rosemary Extract: rich in antioxidants; antimicrobial; helps maintain a healthy digestive tract and immune system.

## Clinical study (third-party veterinary lab, 45 days, dogs switched from competitor foods to Kismet dry food)
- Kismet is clinically proven to improve gut health in dogs.
- 100% of dogs showed improvement in at least one of six gut-health biomarkers.
- Clinically proven to reduce inflammation in the gut.
- 71% of dogs had a decrease in beta-glucuronidase, a sign of better gut bacteria balance.
- Gut microbiome balance and digestion improved for a majority of dogs.
- Approved campaign line used in Kismet's designs: "96% of dogs showed improved gut health after 45 days on Kismet."
- A healthy gut supports digestion, immunity, skin and coat, energy and mood. Signs of gut imbalance: recurring diarrhea, vomiting or gas; itchy or flaky skin, dull coat; low energy.
- Dogs adjust to Kismet within 7–10 days on average. Switch gradually over at least seven days, mixing new food into the old.

## Voice
- Warm, funny, a little cheeky, never cutesy or salesy. Talks like a dog person to dog people: "bowl goals", "nug life", "zoomies", "pet parents".
- Short sentences. Concrete benefits before features. Science in plain words ("without any marketing jargon").
- Say "food for dogs" as often as "dog food". Never claim to cure, treat or prevent disease; use "supports", "helps maintain", "clinically proven to improve gut health".
- Always write in English, whatever language the brief is in.
